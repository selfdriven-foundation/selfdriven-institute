# Appendix A

# The Eight Areas at a Glance

The Eight Areas of Focus are **distinct, persistent, interconnected and non-hierarchical**. They should be applied as a system rather than treated as departments, sequential stages or task categories.

| Area | Purpose | Conducting Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Overall direction, path, intent, design setting and reflection. | **Where are we going, why, and what are we learning as we move?** |
| **Engagement** | Explainability of values, Protocols and Processes — the soul and the story. | **Can participants understand the organisation well enough to participate meaningfully?** |
| **Enablement** | The outputs and products of the organisation. | **What useful things are we actually enabling or producing?** |
| **Protocols** | Standards, technologies, templates and interoperability frameworks. | **What shared rules or interfaces allow coordination without continuous manual intervention?** |
| **Sustainability** | Long-term viability, including capital for sustained human attention and conductors. | **Can this continue without consuming the people, attention or resources that make it possible?** |
| **Processes** | Operational Processes that drive Enablement and organisational functioning. | **How does useful activity reliably happen?** |
| **Accountability** | Internal and external accountability, including sovereign-state regulatory obligations. | **Can we establish what happened, who or what acted, under what authority, and whether obligations were met?** |
| **Organisational** | Coordination and synchronisation across Areas of Focus, including team alignment. | **How do the Areas remain coordinated as one system?** |

These definitions follow the framework's canonical summary.

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Tasks as control | Tasks as implementation |
| Instructions | Direction |
| Communication | Engagement |

The leadership move is **up an abstraction layer**: less unnecessary management intervention, not less organisational discipline.

# Appendix B

# The Conductor's Field Guide

When the management reflex appears, move up one level.

### When you want to assign another task

Ask:

> What organisational condition needs to change?

### When you want another status report

Ask:

> Why isn't relevant organisational state already observable?

### When people repeatedly need instruction

Ask:

> Is Direction or Engagement insufficient?

### When the same question keeps reaching a manager

Ask:

> Should this become a Protocol?

### When another approval is proposed

Ask:

> What risk is this approval controlling, and can that risk be handled structurally?

### When a meeting keeps returning

Ask:

> Which missing coordination function is the meeting currently providing?

### When management wants more visibility

Ask:

> What actually needs to be verifiable?

### When people seem overloaded

Ask:

> Which scarce attention is the system unnecessarily consuming?

### When the same exception keeps occurring

Ask:

> Is it still an exception, or does the Process no longer match reality?

### When teams blame one another

Ask:

> Which relationship or Area is out of synchronisation?

### When one person becomes indispensable

Ask:

> Which organisational context exists only inside this person?

### When an AI system needs constant review

Ask:

> Which Direction, Protocol, Process or Accountability condition is insufficient for bounded agency?

### When AI produces more information

Ask:

> Does this reduce attention demand or simply generate more things for humans to inspect?

### When you are unsure where leadership should focus

Ask:

> **What needs our attention now?**

# Appendix C

# The Conducting Diagnostic

Use this when a recurring organisational tension appears.

## Step 1 — Name the visible problem

Examples:

too many approvals;

slow delivery;

repeated meetings;

conflicting teams;

managers checking everything;

people waiting for instructions;

AI requiring constant supervision.

Describe the symptom without assuming the cause.

---

## Step 2 — Identify the management dependency

Ask:

> What repeated human intervention is currently holding the system together?

Then ask:

> What would fail if that intervention disappeared tomorrow?

This reveals the function the intervention is performing.

---

## Step 3 — Map the Eight Areas

For each Area, identify only what is materially relevant.

**Direction**  
Is intent or priority unclear?

**Engagement**  
Is meaning, rationale or organisational story misunderstood?

**Enablement**  
Is useful outcome being confused with activity?

**Protocols**  
Is repeated interaction dependent on permission or folklore?

**Sustainability**  
Is the system consuming unsustainable attention or resources?

**Processes**  
Where is recurring activity unnecessarily difficult?

**Accountability**  
Can material action, authority and evidence be established?

**Organisational**  
Which dependencies or Areas are out of synchronisation?

Do not manufacture a problem in all eight Areas.

The framework specifically advises against forcing every Area to contain a problem.

---

## Step 4 — Identify the Areas needing attention now

Do not rank the permanent importance of the Areas.

Ask:

> Which one or two conditions are creating the greatest leverage or pressure now?

Attention moves.

The framework persists.

---

## Step 5 — Move the intervention upward

Replace repeated activity-level control where possible with stronger conditions.

From:

> Manager approves every normal customer response.

Towards:

> Clear Direction, Engagement principles, Protocols, escalation boundaries and Accountability evidence allow authorised people to respond.

From:

> Produce another weekly status report.

Towards:

> Make organisational state observable so it does not need to be manually reconstructed.

---

## Step 6 — Release bounded agency

Identify what can now happen without central instruction.

Define:

normal action;

authority;

limits;

evidence;

escalation.

---

## Step 7 — Observe and reflect

Look for:

fewer unnecessary approvals;

less waiting;

less repeated clarification;

lower attention cost;

better traceability;

higher-quality escalation;

improved Enablement;

greater local agency;

continued coherence.

These closely follow the reflection signals in the underlying framework.

# Appendix D

# The Conducting Canvas

## Current Tension

**What is happening?**

**Where is management intervention repeatedly required?**

**What function is that intervention currently providing?**

---

## Direction

What are we trying to make possible?

What assumptions matter?

What has changed?

---

## Engagement

What do participants need to understand?

Why does the current structure exist?

What values or principles matter when judgement is required?

---

## Enablement

What useful output, capability or outcome should exist?

Who or what should be enabled?

---

## Protocols

Which shared rules, standards, interfaces, credentials or authority boundaries are required?

Where can repeated permission disappear?

---

## Sustainability

Which scarce resources are consumed?

How much human attention does the current model require?

Can it scale?

---

## Processes

How should normal useful activity happen?

Which hand-offs are unnecessary?

Which recurring exceptions should become part of the normal Process?

---

## Accountability

What needs to be verifiable?

What authority applies?

Which evidence should arise naturally as activity occurs?

---

## Organisational

Which actors and Areas must remain synchronised?

Where are dependencies, timing or assumptions conflicting?

---

## Agency

What can now occur locally?

What can occur automatically?

What remains explicitly human?

What must escalate?

---

## Reflection

How will we know whether the redesign worked?

**Less management intervention?**

**Better Enablement?**

**Stronger evidence?**

**Lower attention cost?**

**Greater agency?**

**Continued coherence?**

# Appendix E

# The 90-Day Transition Playbook

## Days 1–30 — See the System

### Week 1 — Choose one dependency

Select one recurring:

approval;

meeting;

report;

escalation;

management review;

indispensable person;

human review loop around AI.

Keep it observable.

### Week 2 — Understand its function

Ask:

What would break if this disappeared?

What information does it provide?

What risk does it control?

What authority does it legitimise?

### Week 3 — Map the Eight Areas

Identify the system condition beneath the intervention.

### Week 4 — Measure attention

Measure not only execution time but:

waiting;

clarifying;

reviewing;

chasing;

reconstructing.

---

## Days 31–60 — Redesign the Conditions

### Weeks 5–6 — Strengthen shared context

Clarify Direction.

Improve Engagement.

Make relevant context accessible.

Define the useful Enablement sought.

### Weeks 7–8 — Strengthen architecture

Clarify authority.

Improve Protocols.

Simplify Processes.

Create appropriate Accountability evidence.

Improve Organisational synchronisation.

Protect Sustainability.

---

## Days 61–90 — Release Agency

### Week 9 — Remove one routine intervention

Inside clearly defined boundaries, allow normal activity to proceed without the old control.

### Week 10 — Observe

Do not restore the old control simply because something unexpected occurs.

Diagnose the failure.

Direction?

Engagement?

Capability?

Protocol?

Process?

Accountability?

Organisational coordination?

### Week 11 — Improve the condition

Fix the cause rather than merely controlling the incident.

### Week 12 — Expand

Choose the next management dependency.

Repeat.

---

## The 90-Day Test

At the end of the cycle ask:

**Can normal action occur with less central instruction?**

**Has Accountability remained strong or improved?**

**Has human attention demand fallen?**

**Have exceptions become more meaningful?**

**Has useful Enablement improved?**

**Has coherence held?**

If so, the organisation has not merely become more efficient.

It has become more capable.
