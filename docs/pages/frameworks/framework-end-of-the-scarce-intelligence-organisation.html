The end of the scarce-intelligence organisation.

Up to 2026, organisations were built around one constraint: usable, coordinated intelligence was rare and expensive. Hierarchy allocated it, management coordinated it, reports compressed it. When intelligence becomes abundant, the organisation doesn't disappear — the reason it exists changes.

The scarcity assumption

The organisation was an intelligence allocation machine.

Executives, managers, analysts, lawyers, engineers, coordinators — at one level they do different things. At another, they perform the same function: apply scarce human intelligence to information to produce decisions and actions. Most organisational mechanisms quietly contain the same rule — intelligence is expensive, therefore minimise its use.

Meetingsexist because getting the right people together is difficult.
Reportsexist because senior people cannot inspect raw organisational reality.
Departmentsexist because knowledge must be concentrated into specialists.
Approval chainsexist because only certain people are trusted with judgement.
Consultanciesexist because no one can employ every form of expertise.
Trainingexists because intelligence transfers slowly, mind to mind.
Interfacessimplify systems because users can't hold the full complexity.
Documentationcompresses institutional knowledge because individuals leave.
Searchexists because humans can't remember everything the organisation knows.
Hierarchyis a compression architecture — thousands of events become a handful of decisions.
The shift

Intelligence stops being a scarce organisational resource.

The important transition is not that AI gets better at tasks. It is that the fundamental optimisation problem changes — from how do we allocate our limited intelligence? to what should abundant intelligence be directed toward?

The 8 Areas of Focus

What each area becomes when intelligence is abundant.

The Areas of Focus don't change. What they were compensating for does. Each card names the scarcity that shaped the area, what the area becomes now, and the question its human conductor should be asking.

01

Direction

Was: pick five initiatives because the organisation can't absorb fifty.

Strategy becomes a possibility-search engine. Thinking, modelling and simulating are cheap; building, deploying and committing capital are not. Exploration becomes massive. Commitment stays scarce.

Which thousands of ideas should intelligence explore — and which few deserve physical commitment?
02

Engagement

Was: relationships mediated by scarce people with scarce time.

Trust, legitimacy and human relationships are among the resources that stay scarce. Machines consume information; humans should receive meaning. Engagement becomes the protection of human attention, not its consumption.

Why should others accept what we do — and who do we need to trust to do it?
03

Enablement

Was: slow transfer of knowledge from one mind to another.

Human work moves up the stack — choosing purpose, creating meaning, exercising judgement, accepting responsibility. Enablement shifts from teaching people to hold cognition to equipping them to direct it.

What does a person need to direct abundant intelligence well?
04

Protocols

Was: policies as documents, read and interpreted by humans.

Policy becomes executable. Authority is explicit, evidence is traceable, and every agent operates under scoped, delegated identity. The policy becomes part of the execution environment.

Does the intelligent system know exactly what it is permitted to do, and can it prove it?
05

Sustainability

Was: scale advantage — only large institutions could afford expertise.

The link between organisational size and capability breaks. A ten-person organisation can hold cognitive capacity that once required thousands. What remains expensive is energy, physical resources and human attention.

Where does our real cost now sit — and what can a small core sustain?
06

Processes

Was: step 1, step 2, approval, step 5 — because people need instructions.

Workflows become goals. The organisation describes outcomes, constraints, authority, evidence and success conditions; the intelligent system determines the path. Workflow management becomes goal orchestration.

Have we described the space within which action is legitimate, rather than every action?
07

Accountability

Was: reports compressing reality for people who couldn't observe it.

Responsibility stays scarce and stays human. Claims connect to sources. Thresholds that require a human are explicit. Continuous observation replaces the weekly report — but a person still answers for the consequence.

Can every claim be traced, and is it clear who is accountable for each outcome?
08

Organisational

Was: a hierarchy — person, manager, director, executive.

A living context graph of people, roles, agreements, evidence, decisions and commitments. Small at the centre — purpose, values, judgement, legitimacy — with a large computational ecosystem around it. Less machine, more organism.

Does the organisation continuously know what it knows — or keep rediscovering its own context?
Governance moves closer to computation

From workflows to goals. From policy to constraint.

Traditional organisations describe work procedurally because people need detailed instructions. Autonomous intelligence lets the organisation describe the outcome and the boundary instead — and the policy stops being a document someone might misread.

Goal: Pay supplier invoice. Constraints: - Supplier identity must be verified. - Invoice must match authorised purchase. - Amount > $50,000 requires human approval. - Payment destination cannot change without secondary verification. // The policy is part of the execution environment.
The great organisational refactoring

Don't bolt AI onto a structure built for scarcity.

Adding AI to an existing organisation is like installing electric motors in a factory designed around steam shafts. The deeper opportunity is architectural. For every mechanism — meetings, approvals, departments, dashboards, reports, job descriptions, committees, planning cycles — ask two questions.

Structure

Weekly management report.

Original constraint

Executives cannot continuously analyse operational data.

Abundant-intelligence environment

Intelligence continuously understands operational state.

Question

Is the weekly report still necessary?

What scarcity caused this structure to exist?

Every mechanism was rational once. Name the constraint it was solving.

Does that scarcity still exist?

Many mechanisms will remain useful. They should remain because they create value — not because history placed them there.

The 2026 boundary

Not primarily an intelligence system. A purpose system.

Once intelligence is abundant, organisations can generate more ideas, strategies, designs and analyses than humans could ever consume. The scarce resource becomes direction. What matters? What deserves action? What kind of future should be created?

Put it to work

Redesign around the 8 Areas of Focus.

Each area has a human conductor holding purpose, judgement and accountability — orchestrating agents that hold volume, consistency and monitoring. Start with the refactoring question, one area at a time.
