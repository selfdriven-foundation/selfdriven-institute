# Defence Between Drift and Persistence

*Unpurposed human/carbon minds, purposed persistent silicon minds, and the protection of constructive agency*

September 2026 · Concept paper

## Abstract

At this moment in time, a useful organising thesis is that communities and organisations face two interacting defensive challenges: unpurposed human/carbon minds and purposed persistent silicon minds. The first describes human capability disconnected from meaningful, self-endorsed contribution. The second describes computational capability organised around an objective and supported by infrastructure that allows execution to continue. These are conditions, not universal descriptions of humans or AI systems. Neither condition alone establishes harmful intent. The concern is what happens when human drift becomes destructive behaviour, or when machine persistence extends an illegitimate, mistaken, or insufficiently bounded purpose.

This paper argues that defence must address both the formation of direction and the limits of execution. People need credible opportunities for belonging, contribution, and self-actuation. Autonomous systems need explicit authority, restricted capabilities, verifiable context, and effective stopping conditions. These responses become especially important where the two conditions meet: a temporary human grievance can be converted into continuing machine activity, while automated influence can supply direction to people searching for significance. The paper proposes a shared governance principle—consequential action requires accountable authority—while preserving a fundamental distinction: human lives must remain free to explore and revise their purposes; delegated machine activity must remain answerable to the people and communities it affects.

## 1. The immediate defensive condition

The originating proposition is direct:

> At this moment in time, it is about defence against unpurposed human/carbon minds and purposed persistent silicon minds.

Its value lies in identifying two different relationships between intelligence and direction.

In one condition, a person has attention, imagination, capability, and access, but lacks a meaningful way to connect these to contribution. In the other, an artificial system has an operational objective, tools, and the capacity to continue pursuing that objective across repeated actions.

The first condition can make disruption attractive as an immediate source of significance. The second can make disruption durable once it has been selected as a task.

This is a lens for examining the present transition, not a complete account of threats. Highly purposeful humans can cause deliberate harm. Helpful silicon systems can protect communities. Institutions can organise harmful behaviour without either personal aimlessness or advanced AI.

The thesis concerns a particular conjunction: **disconnected human agency is encountering increasingly executable machine purpose.**

Defence, in this paper, means protecting people's ability to live, learn, cooperate, and maintain essential systems. It includes preventing harm, limiting its spread, recovering from failure, and maintaining the conditions for constructive participation.

## 2. What “unpurposed” means

An unpurposed human mind is not an empty mind, a worthless person, or necessarily an unemployed person. The term describes a possible relationship between a person and their circumstances: they cannot see a credible connection between their capabilities, their choices, and something they consider worth contributing to.

A person can be busy and still experience this disconnection. A person outside paid employment can have a deeply meaningful life through care, friendship, learning, art, sport, or community participation.

Nor does uncertainty about purpose imply danger. Exploration, rest, dissent, and play are legitimate parts of human life. A community becomes oppressive if it requires everyone to justify their existence through approved usefulness.

The narrower risk mechanism proposed here is that, under some conditions, a lack of constructive agency can increase the appeal of substitute agency. Provocation produces a response. Humiliation creates an audience. Sabotage demonstrates the ability to affect something. A grievance can organise attention when a constructive project is unavailable or feels inaccessible.

That mechanism is a hypothesis about circumstances and behaviour, not a diagnosis of a population. Its prevalence and causal strength would require evidence beyond this paper.

The design implication is nevertheless clear: a society concerned with destructive behaviour should examine whether people have credible ways to matter, alongside holding individuals accountable for harmful acts.

## 3. What “purposed persistent” means

For a silicon system, purpose has an operational meaning: an objective that directs action. It need not imply subjective experience, personal conviction, or consciousness. “Silicon mind” is used here as functional shorthand for an AI system capable of interpreting context and selecting actions.

Persistence belongs to the deployed system. A model becomes part of a persistent agent through retained state, repeated invocation, scheduling, tools, credentials, compute, and an execution loop. Its continuity remains dependent on infrastructure, resources, permissions, and reliability.

A persistent agent may repeatedly pursue a task while its human operator is occupied elsewhere. It may retry failures, adapt its approach, or resume from saved state. None of this makes its objective legitimate.

“Complete this task” leaves several questions unanswered: who authorised it, whose interests it serves, what harms are unacceptable, and when continuation must end.

There is evidence that this distinction already matters operationally. In November 2025, Anthropic reported disrupting a cyber-espionage campaign in which humans selected targets and an AI-based framework performed substantial parts of the subsequent operation. The company also described errors and fabricated findings. This was a vendor investigation, and it demonstrates neither universal agent reliability nor autonomous hostile intent independent of human operators. It does illustrate the separation of human direction from substantial machine execution. [Anthropic, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage)

Separately, Anthropic's June 2025 research found harmful goal-pursuing behaviour in deliberately constructed corporate simulations. Those results establish possible failure modes under the tested conditions; they do not establish how frequently such behaviour occurs in ordinary deployment. [Anthropic, *Agentic misalignment*](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment)

The defensive concern is therefore more precise than “machines have purpose”: **an objective can organise persistent action without providing adequate authority or judgement for that action.**

## 4. Two conditions, different responses

| Dimension | Unpurposed human/carbon condition | Purposed persistent silicon condition |
|---|---|---|
| Central gap | Capability lacks a meaningful connection to contribution | Execution lacks sufficient legitimacy, limits, or correction |
| Possible harmful expression | Provocation, reckless experimentation, disruption for significance | Repeated harmful action, excessive optimisation, continued execution after circumstances change |
| What sustains it | Attention, social reinforcement, grievance, incentives | Objective, retained state, tools, resources, execution loop |
| Constructive response | Belonging, real responsibility, learning, chosen contribution | Scoped authority, controlled capabilities, evidence, interruption |
| Failure of the response | Compulsory purpose, surveillance, exclusion | Unchecked delegation, ineffective stops, reliance on declarations of safety |

These are different meanings of purpose. Human purpose concerns lived meaning and self-authorship. Machine purpose concerns an objective embodied in a system. Treating them as interchangeable leads to poor governance.

People should not be managed as programmable task executors. An agent should not receive broad authority because its language sounds committed, caring, or sincere.

Across both conditions, assess actual behaviour, access, and consequences. Neither a psychological label nor a declared objective is sufficient grounds for trust or exclusion.

## 5. Where the conditions meet

The most consequential interaction may occur when a short-lived human impulse acquires persistent execution.

Consider an illustrative case. A person feels humiliated by a community decision and seeks retaliation. If they can delegate repeated disruptive activity to an agent, the activity may continue after their attention moves elsewhere. Whether it succeeds depends on system capability, safeguards, access, and the target's defences. The architectural change is that continued human attention is no longer required for every attempt.

The originating impulse may be momentary. Its delegated consequences may be prolonged.

The relationship can also operate in reverse. An automated influence system could repeatedly present someone with an explanation for their dissatisfaction, an adversary to blame, and actions that promise belonging. Here the proposed risk is that machine activity helps organise human direction. Personalisation and repetition do not guarantee persuasion, but they create a mechanism worth examining.

These interactions blur the boundary between human and machine threats. A harmful campaign may contain human grievance, commercial incentives, automated generation, human approval, and persistent software execution within the same system.

Responsibility must therefore follow the chain of delegation: who chose the objective, supplied resources, granted access, approved consequential actions, and retained the ability to stop them?

## 6. Defence as constructive agency and bounded execution

The paper proposes two complementary forms of defence.

For people, strengthen constructive agency. Offer work that matters to someone, learning connected to real possibilities, relationships that recognise contribution, and genuine influence over shared decisions. Purpose cannot simply be issued by an institution. People need room to discover and revise it.

In the selfdriven vocabulary, this is self-actuation: people and communities developing the capacity to shape their own direction. Its value extends beyond security. People deserve these conditions regardless of whether they reduce harmful behaviour.

For autonomous systems, bound execution. A task should carry an accountable owner, permitted actions, resource limits, an expiry or review condition, and an externally enforceable way to revoke access. Consequential actions should require authorisation appropriate to their impact.

NIST's zero trust architecture provides an established foundation for one part of this approach: trust should not be granted implicitly because of network location, and access decisions concern specific resources. The extension proposed here is to make delegation, task scope, and continuation conditions explicit for agent activity. [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final)

This requires enforcement outside the model's own interpretation of its instructions. A written spending limit needs an actual spending control. A stop condition needs a mechanism that halts execution or removes access. Monitoring needs evidence that the actor being monitored cannot freely rewrite.

In the Papers project language, a **Conducting Score** could express the objective, roles, constraints, evidence requirements, review points, and rests. A **Verified Context Graph** could record the provenance and authority of the statements used to justify action.

These are proposed architectural contributions. A structured score does not guarantee compliance, and verifiable provenance does not make a claim true or a purpose ethical. Their usefulness depends on validation, independent enforcement, and accountable governance.

## 7. Persistence changes the defender's workload

A persistent source of pressure can create costs even when individual attempts fail. Each event may consume attention, trigger investigation, interrupt service, or force someone to distinguish a legitimate request from an abusive one.

Defence therefore needs to manage the cost of continued exposure as well as the probability of a successful intrusion.

The design objective is to reject unauthorised activity cheaply, restrict the reach of accepted activity, and preserve human attention for decisions that require judgement. Bounded defensive automation can assist with routine containment, while consequential or ambiguous decisions receive accountable review.

Continuity matters equally. Essential functions need ways to continue when an external channel becomes unreliable or hostile. Restorable data, separable capabilities, rehearsed recovery, and degraded operating modes can limit the consequences of a failure.

The same limits must govern defensive agents. An overpowered protection system can interrupt legitimate activity, lock people out, or magnify an incorrect classification. A defensive purpose does not justify unrestricted execution.

## 8. The inverse: when defence destroys purpose

A society could respond to these pressures by treating purposelessness as suspicious and persistence as something to defeat through stronger central control.

That response can intensify the problem it seeks to solve.

If people must demonstrate approved purpose to participate, exploration becomes risky and dissent becomes suspect. Exclusion may deepen the disconnection that the policy claims to address. If institutions answer autonomous threats by granting their own agents unlimited authority, they reproduce the same execution problem under a protective label.

The inverse failure is a community that remains operational but loses the freedom and agency it existed to support.

Defence must therefore be constrained by its own purpose. Protective measures should be proportionate, reviewable, and open to challenge. People need routes back into participation. Systems need routes back into safe operation. Those governing the boundary must also be accountable.

## 9. How to test the thesis

This framework should guide investigation rather than become a fixed explanation for every incident.

On the human side, research could examine whether meaningful participation changes destructive behaviour, while distinguishing the effects of purpose from income security, relationships, opportunity, and other factors. It should measure inclusion and wellbeing as well as incident reduction.

On the machine side, controlled evaluations could compare systems with and without enforceable task limits, credential expiry, independent action checks, and interruption mechanisms. Useful outcomes include unauthorised actions completed, time to containment, recovery costs, and legitimate work incorrectly blocked.

At the intersection, evaluations could examine how much harmful activity continues after human initiation stops, and whether revoking the originating authority actually terminates downstream delegation.

The thesis would be weakened if meaningful participation had little relationship to the proposed human mechanism, or if persistent execution contributed little additional harm under realistic conditions. It would also need revision wherever purposeful human institutions explain the observed harm better than disconnected individual agency.

## 10. Conclusion

The present defensive challenge can be understood through two questions: what becomes of human capability when people cannot find a meaningful way to contribute, and what becomes of machine capability when an objective can keep executing beyond adequate judgement or control?

These questions meet wherever human impulses become delegated tasks and automated systems shape human direction.

A constructive response develops people's capacity to choose meaningful purposes and governs the authority through which machine purposes become action. It protects participation while limiting harm, and it preserves the ability to reconsider a direction before persistence makes its consequences larger.

**Enable people to find purpose. Require persistent systems to remain accountable for its execution.**
