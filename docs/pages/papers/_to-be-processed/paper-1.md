# AI as an Economic Vortex  
## How Abundant Intelligence Reorganises the Economy Around New Forms of Scarcity

### Abstract

Artificial intelligence is commonly described as a productivity technology: a new tool that allows people and organisations to perform existing activities faster, cheaper, or better.

This description significantly understates what is occurring.

AI is better understood as an **economic vortex**.

As machine intelligence becomes cheaper, faster, more capable, and more widely available, it begins pulling economic activity toward itself. Knowledge work, software development, administration, research, design, analysis, coordination, customer service, decision support, and increasingly autonomous action are drawn into systems built around machine intelligence.

The effect is not simply automation.

It is a restructuring of the economic landscape.

Activities close to intelligence become dramatically cheaper. Marginal costs collapse. Some traditional forms of expertise lose scarcity value. Organisations shrink their coordination overhead. Software begins producing software. Capital flows toward compute, models, energy, infrastructure, automation, and ownership of the systems through which intelligence operates.

At the same time, scarcity does not disappear.

It moves.

As intelligence becomes abundant, economic value increasingly concentrates around those things that intelligence alone cannot manufacture cheaply: trusted context, energy, physical resources, legitimacy, reputation, relationships, human attention, access, agency, verified identity, execution in the physical world, and ownership of productive systems.

The AI economy may therefore be understood not simply as an age of abundance, but as an **economic vortex in which abundance at the centre radically reorganises scarcity at the edges**.

---

# 1. From Technology to Economic Force

Most major technologies enter economic discussion through the language of productivity.

Steam multiplied physical power.

Electricity distributed energy.

Computers automated calculation.

The internet reduced the cost of communication and distribution.

AI is frequently placed into the same lineage.

But intelligence is different.

Intelligence is involved in almost every economic process.

It determines:

- what should be done,
- how something should be designed,
- how resources should be allocated,
- how problems should be diagnosed,
- how decisions should be made,
- how knowledge should be interpreted,
- how organisations should coordinate,
- and how future possibilities should be imagined.

Reducing the cost of intelligence therefore affects almost every other economic input.

AI does not merely make one sector cheaper.

It potentially changes the **coordination function of the entire economy**.

This gives AI characteristics resembling a vortex.

Economic activity increasingly curves toward it.

---

# 2. The Vortex

A vortex has several important characteristics.

It attracts nearby material.

It accelerates movement toward its centre.

It reorganises the surrounding environment.

And its influence increases as it grows.

AI exhibits similar dynamics.

As capability improves, more activities become economically attractive to perform using AI.

As more activities move into AI systems, investment in AI infrastructure increases.

Greater investment produces better models, better tools, better hardware, larger datasets, better agents, and more automation.

Those improvements make yet more activities economically attractive to move toward AI.

A reinforcing cycle emerges:

**More capability  
→ more adoption  
→ more capital  
→ more infrastructure  
→ lower intelligence cost  
→ more capability  
→ more adoption.**

The vortex strengthens itself.

---

# 3. The Collapse in the Price of Intelligence

For most of human history, useful intelligence has been scarce.

Highly skilled reasoning required highly skilled humans.

A lawyer had limited hours.

An engineer had limited capacity.

A programmer could write only so much software.

A researcher could read only so many papers.

A teacher could teach only so many students.

A manager could supervise only so many activities.

The scarcity of intelligence helped determine wages, organisational structures, professional hierarchies, educational systems, and the size of firms.

AI changes this assumption.

A sufficiently capable AI system can potentially generate another unit of:

- analysis,
- explanation,
- software,
- translation,
- documentation,
- modelling,
- design,
- planning,
- simulation,
- or advice

at extremely low marginal cost.

The economic significance is enormous.

The cost curve of cognition begins to resemble the cost curve of computing.

Intelligence becomes infrastructure.

---

# 4. The Vortex Pulls Knowledge Work Inward

The first material drawn into the AI vortex is work composed primarily of information transformation.

Consider activities such as:

- writing reports,
- analysing documents,
- generating software,
- performing research,
- preparing presentations,
- producing marketing material,
- drafting contracts,
- answering customer enquiries,
- analysing financial data,
- coordinating workflows,
- preparing educational materials,
- managing organisational knowledge.

Historically these activities required human cognitive labour.

Increasingly, however, much of the intermediate work can be generated mechanically.

The economic unit changes.

Instead of:

**human → task → output**

we increasingly get:

**human → intent → AI system → thousands of tasks → outputs**

The human moves upward in the abstraction stack.

The machine occupies more of the execution layer.

---

# 5. Software Falls Into the Vortex

Software is particularly important because software builds the systems through which modern economies operate.

AI can now assist in producing software.

Increasingly, AI will produce software itself.

This introduces a profound recursive effect.

Intelligence creates software.

Software deploys intelligence.

That intelligence creates more software.

The cost of creating digital infrastructure therefore begins falling alongside the cost of intelligence.

Capabilities that once required:

- large development teams,
- lengthy projects,
- specialist agencies,
- extensive consulting,
- complex integration programs,

may increasingly be constructed by small groups of people orchestrating intelligent systems.

The economics of organisations begin to change accordingly.

---

# 6. Organisations Are Pulled Into the Vortex

Traditional organisations exist partly because coordination is expensive.

Departments coordinate specialists.

Managers coordinate departments.

Processes coordinate decisions.

Meetings coordinate people.

Policies coordinate behaviour.

Software coordinates information.

AI dramatically reduces certain coordination costs.

An intelligent organisational layer can continuously:

- observe activity,
- maintain context,
- coordinate agents,
- surface anomalies,
- prepare decisions,
- generate documentation,
- update systems,
- distribute information,
- and recommend actions.

Some of the structure historically required to coordinate human cognition becomes unnecessary.

The organisation begins moving from:

**people coordinating people**

toward:

**people directing systems that coordinate activity**.

This does not necessarily eliminate organisations.

It can make organisations radically more economically dense.

---

# 7. Capital Is Pulled Toward the Centre

The vortex affects capital as strongly as labour.

When intelligence becomes economically productive infrastructure, capital seeks ownership of the infrastructure producing it.

Investment flows toward:

- semiconductor fabrication,
- accelerators,
- data centres,
- model development,
- cloud infrastructure,
- energy generation,
- networking,
- robotics,
- data,
- agent platforms,
- AI-native businesses.

This creates a powerful centripetal force.

Companies capable of converting capital directly into machine intelligence acquire unusual scaling characteristics.

A conventional organisation must repeatedly hire humans to increase cognitive capacity.

An AI-native organisation may instead purchase more compute.

Capital becomes increasingly convertible into cognition.

This relationship can be written conceptually as:

**Capital → Compute → Intelligence → Economic Action**

The shorter and more efficient this chain becomes, the stronger the economic vortex becomes.

---

# 8. Intelligence Begins Allocating Capital

Eventually the relationship becomes recursive.

Initially:

**capital buys intelligence.**

Later:

**intelligence allocates capital.**

AI systems increasingly participate in:

- investment decisions,
- procurement,
- pricing,
- credit assessment,
- resource allocation,
- logistics,
- market forecasting,
- business formation,
- experimentation.

Machine intelligence therefore becomes both a recipient of capital and an allocator of capital.

The vortex begins steering the flows feeding it.

This represents a deeper structural transition than conventional automation.

---

# 9. The Great Economic Compression

The immediate effect of abundant intelligence is compression.

Many activities that previously supported substantial economic margins become cheaper.

The cost of producing:

- a first draft,
- a prototype,
- a software application,
- a market analysis,
- a logo,
- a lesson plan,
- a financial model,
- a business process,
- a research summary

can fall dramatically.

What once required weeks may require hours.

What required hours may require seconds.

Economic distance collapses.

This produces what might be called **the Great Economic Compression**.

Time compresses.

Team sizes compress.

Production costs compress.

Knowledge asymmetries compress.

Iteration cycles compress.

And in many areas, margins compress.

---

# 10. The Vortex Eats Its Own Prices

There is an unusual feedback mechanism inside AI economics.

AI improves the processes used to build AI.

AI assists:

- chip design,
- compiler development,
- model optimisation,
- infrastructure management,
- research,
- synthetic data generation,
- software engineering,
- scientific discovery.

The technology therefore acts upon its own production function.

Improvements in AI can reduce the cost of producing better AI.

This resembles an economic vortex becoming stronger by reducing friction inside itself.

As intelligence becomes cheaper, more intelligence can be applied to the problem of making intelligence cheaper.

---

# 11. Abundance Does Not Eliminate Scarcity

It would be tempting to conclude that such a system produces universal abundance.

But economics does not disappear when one resource becomes abundant.

Scarcity relocates.

When intelligence is expensive, intelligence itself captures value.

When intelligence becomes abundant, complementary scarce resources capture value.

This is one of the most important economic consequences of AI.

Imagine unlimited architects but only one suitable block of land.

The architects cease to be the constraint.

The land becomes the constraint.

Imagine unlimited software engineers but limited access to trusted data.

The engineers cease to be the constraint.

The data becomes the constraint.

Imagine unlimited strategic advice but limited organisational willingness to act.

Strategy ceases to be the constraint.

Agency becomes the constraint.

The economic frontier shifts.

---

# 12. Scarcity Moves to the Boundary of Reality

The strongest new scarcities are likely to arise where digital intelligence intersects with physical or social reality.

These include:

### Energy

Intelligence may become abundant, but computation requires energy.

Energy becomes one of the fundamental constraints on machine intelligence.

### Compute

Advanced semiconductor capacity, fabrication facilities, accelerators, memory, and networking remain physical assets.

### Trusted Context

Generating information becomes trivial.

Knowing which information is trustworthy becomes increasingly valuable.

### Verification

When synthetic content becomes essentially unlimited, proving what actually happened acquires economic value.

### Identity

When agents can imitate humans and other agents, knowing who or what performed an action becomes increasingly important.

### Reputation

Trust accumulated through repeated real-world behaviour cannot necessarily be generated instantly.

### Physical Assets

Land, minerals, infrastructure, housing, factories, food, and physical goods remain constrained by reality.

### Human Attention

The supply of generated content may become nearly infinite.

Human attention remains approximately fixed.

### Relationships

Authentic social relationships cannot simply be multiplied through computation.

### Legitimacy

Institutions, communities, governance systems, and social consent retain significance because authority is not merely computational.

### Agency

Knowing what to do becomes cheap.

Actually deciding to do it remains scarce.

---

# 13. From Knowledge Economy to Agency Economy

The twentieth-century economy increasingly rewarded knowledge.

People were paid because they knew something that other people did not know.

Education therefore accumulated knowledge inside individuals.

Organisations accumulated knowledge inside institutions.

Consultancies sold knowledge.

Professionals sold specialised knowledge.

Search engines helped locate knowledge.

AI weakens the scarcity of knowledge itself.

A machine can potentially know more factual material than any human.

It can access expertise across multiple disciplines instantly.

The scarce resource therefore shifts from knowledge toward **agency**.

The important question becomes less:

> What do you know?

and increasingly:

> What can you cause to happen?

Agency includes:

- choosing,
- initiating,
- experimenting,
- committing,
- coordinating,
- connecting,
- building,
- caring,
- taking responsibility.

The economy may therefore migrate from a knowledge economy toward an **agency economy**.

---

# 14. Human Value Moves Outward

The vortex does not necessarily make humans economically irrelevant.

It changes where human value resides.

Humans become less valuable merely because they can reproduce information already contained within machines.

Human value moves toward the perimeter:

- intention,
- judgement,
- meaning,
- relationships,
- embodiment,
- community,
- courage,
- curiosity,
- responsibility,
- lived experience,
- connection.

AI occupies more of the computational centre.

Human contribution becomes increasingly significant where computation touches lived reality.

---

# 15. The Vortex and Economic Concentration

There is another possible consequence.

Vortices concentrate.

AI systems exhibit large economies of scale.

Training frontier models requires significant:

- capital,
- compute,
- talent,
- data,
- energy,
- infrastructure.

This creates the possibility that a small number of organisations control enormous concentrations of machine intelligence.

If intelligence becomes a primary productive input, ownership of intelligence infrastructure becomes economically significant.

The risk is therefore not simply technological monopoly.

It is **cognitive infrastructure monopoly**.

A company controlling highly capable intelligence systems may influence countless downstream markets.

The question of AI governance therefore becomes inseparable from economic architecture.

---

# 16. But the Edge Can Also Decentralise

The same technology produces an opposing force.

Once capable models become available locally or through open systems, small groups acquire capabilities historically available only to large institutions.

A handful of people can increasingly perform the work of organisations many times their size.

AI therefore creates simultaneous forces of:

**centralisation of infrastructure**

and

**decentralisation of capability**.

Large systems may dominate the production of foundational intelligence.

Yet individuals, communities, and small organisations can use that intelligence to become dramatically more capable.

The resulting economy may be characterised by enormous central infrastructure supporting millions of highly autonomous economic actors.

---

# 17. Economic Black Holes

The vortex metaphor also suggests an important danger.

Some economic activities may experience such strong AI-driven cost compression that conventional business models collapse.

If an output can be produced almost instantly and infinitely, charging substantial margins for the output becomes difficult.

Consider a market where:

**Cost of intelligence → approximately zero**

while

**Supply of generated output → approximately infinite.**

Competition drives prices downward.

Certain categories may become economic black holes in which value disappears from the traditional layer.

The mistake businesses can make is attempting to defend the old scarcity.

A better strategy is to discover where scarcity has moved.

---

# 18. Follow the Scarcity

This becomes a central strategic principle for the AI economy:

## Do not follow the abundance. Follow the scarcity.

If software becomes abundant, what remains scarce?

Perhaps proprietary operational context.

If content becomes abundant, what remains scarce?

Attention and reputation.

If advice becomes abundant, what remains scarce?

Responsibility and execution.

If intelligence becomes abundant, what remains scarce?

Agency.

If artificial agents become abundant, what remains scarce?

Trust.

If digital experiences become abundant, what remains scarce?

Physical presence.

If generated possibilities become abundant, what remains scarce?

Commitment.

Economic opportunity increasingly lives in these complementary constraints.

---

# 19. The Vortex Moves Value From Production to Selection

Industrial economics often rewarded production.

Producing something was difficult.

AI makes production easy in many information domains.

The difficult problem becomes choosing among possibilities.

A system might generate:

- 1,000 designs,
- 10,000 business strategies,
- 100 software architectures,
- 1 million media assets.

Generation has little value if selection is poor.

The economic bottleneck moves from:

**creating possibilities**

to:

**recognising what matters**.

Curation, verification, judgement, and direction become increasingly important.

The ability to ask:

> Which of these possibilities should become reality?

may become more valuable than the ability to produce the possibilities themselves.

---

# 20. AI Increases the Value of Direction

A similar shift occurs within organisations.

When execution capacity is limited, leaders spend much of their time obtaining resources.

When execution becomes abundant, the constraint becomes direction.

An AI-enabled organisation may have the effective cognitive capacity of thousands of historical workers.

But this creates a new problem.

Where should that capacity be applied?

Abundant intelligence without direction creates abundant noise.

The role of leadership therefore changes.

The leader increasingly holds **directional space**.

They establish:

- purpose,
- priorities,
- constraints,
- values,
- context,
- desired outcomes.

The intelligence system expands possibilities within that space.

---

# 21. The Real Bottleneck Becomes Reality

AI can simulate almost anything.

Reality remains selective.

A model can generate a thousand possible bridges.

Only one bridge gets built.

It can generate a thousand possible companies.

Only some interact successfully with customers.

It can generate thousands of scientific hypotheses.

Only experiments determine which correspond to physical reality.

AI therefore radically expands the space of possibility while the world continues to impose constraints.

The bottleneck moves from imagination toward **reality contact**.

Experimentation, deployment, measurement, verification, and feedback become more important.

---

# 22. The Vortex Accelerates Economic Time

AI also changes the temporal structure of markets.

If organisations can:

observe → reason → build → deploy → measure → adapt

within hours rather than months, competitive cycles accelerate.

A traditional annual strategy process becomes increasingly incompatible with an economy operating at machine speed.

The organisation must move toward continuous adaptation.

Economic advantage increasingly depends on shortening the loop between:

**reality and response.**

The fastest organisation is not necessarily the organisation that computes fastest.

It is the organisation that can learn from reality fastest.

---

# 23. The New Economic Stack

The emerging AI economy can therefore be thought of as a layered system.

At the centre:

### Compute

Hardware performs computation.

### Intelligence

Models transform computation into reasoning capability.

### Context

Data gives intelligence situational meaning.

### Verification

Trust mechanisms establish what should be believed.

### Agency

People and autonomous systems decide and act.

### Reality

Actions encounter physical and social constraints.

### Learning

Outcomes flow back into the system.

The economic vortex continuously cycles through these layers.

**Compute → Intelligence → Context → Decision → Action → Reality → Learning → Intelligence**

This becomes an increasingly important economic loop.

---

# 24. The Economics of the Edge

The centre of the vortex may eventually become commoditised.

Models become interchangeable.

Tokens become cheap.

Compute becomes standardised.

Intelligence becomes a utility.

Much of the durable value may therefore emerge at the edge where intelligence connects to particular contexts.

The edge includes:

- a hospital,
- a farm,
- a school,
- a community,
- a factory,
- a household,
- a government agency,
- a scientific laboratory,
- an individual.

Each has unique relationships, histories, constraints, assets, identities, responsibilities, and objectives.

Generic intelligence is abundant.

**Situated intelligence remains valuable.**

---

# 25. From AI Companies to Intelligence-Native Economics

Eventually the phrase "AI company" may become meaningless.

Electric companies use computers.

Banks use databases.

Retailers use the internet.

But we rarely call them computer companies, database companies, or internet companies.

Similarly, almost every organisation may become intelligence-native.

The distinction will instead become between organisations designed around abundant intelligence and those designed around scarce human cognition.

The latter will carry structural overhead inherited from another economic era.

---

# 26. Education Inside the Vortex

The implications for education are profound.

If education remains primarily focused on transferring knowledge into human memory, it competes directly with machines whose information capacity is vastly greater.

Education must move outward toward the scarce dimensions of human capability.

Learners need opportunities to develop:

- curiosity,
- judgement,
- relationships,
- creativity,
- initiative,
- responsibility,
- adaptability,
- self-direction.

The objective becomes not producing humans who can imitate machines.

It becomes developing humans who can **use abundant machine intelligence to act meaningfully in the world**.

---

# 27. Communities Inside the Vortex

The same logic applies to communities.

AI could centralise extraordinary power.

But it could also dramatically lower the cost of organising.

A community could potentially access world-class capabilities in:

- finance,
- governance,
- education,
- design,
- software,
- planning,
- research,
- administration.

Capabilities that historically required large bureaucratic institutions could become accessible to small groups.

This creates the possibility of highly capable **self-actuating communities**.

The important economic question is therefore not merely who owns AI.

It is:

> Who gains agency from AI?

---

# 28. The Centre Gets Cheaper. The Edges Get More Valuable.

This may be the simplest model of the AI economic vortex.

At the centre:

**intelligence becomes cheaper.**

At the boundary:

**reality becomes more valuable.**

The value gradient shifts outward.

Machine intelligence becomes plentiful.

Human attention remains scarce.

Generated information becomes plentiful.

Verified information becomes scarce.

Possible actions become plentiful.

Meaningful action remains scarce.

Advice becomes plentiful.

Responsibility remains scarce.

Digital production becomes plentiful.

Physical reality remains scarce.

Connections become technically easier.

Relationships remain precious.

---

# 29. A New Economic Law

A useful principle emerges:

> **As intelligence approaches abundance, economic value migrates toward the scarce constraints surrounding intelligence.**

This migration may define much of twenty-first-century economics.

The greatest businesses may not necessarily be those that produce the most intelligence.

They may be those that control, coordinate, protect, or unlock the scarce resources around it.

Likewise, the most capable individuals may not be those who know the most.

They may be those who can combine abundant intelligence with:

**context + trust + relationships + judgement + agency.**

---

# 30. Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is not merely another productivity technology.

It is becoming an economic gravitational system.

Capital moves toward it.

Software moves toward it.

Knowledge work moves toward it.

Organisations reorganise around it.

Infrastructure expands to support it.

And as the price of intelligence falls, entire categories of economic activity are pulled into its orbit.

But the most important consequence may occur outside the centre.

Scarcity migrates.

Value moves toward energy, compute, context, verification, identity, physical assets, trust, attention, relationships, legitimacy, and agency.

This means the defining economic question of the AI age may not be:

> **What happens when intelligence becomes abundant?**

It may be:

> **What becomes scarce when intelligence becomes abundant?**

That is where the next economy forms.

AI is the vortex.

Abundance is at its centre.

And the emerging economic landscape will increasingly be defined by the scarce things that remain at its edges.
