# Octomics and Scarcity in the Age of Abundance

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is pushing society toward an unusual economic condition: the abundance of capabilities that were historically scarce.

Knowledge, computation, analysis, software, design, translation, explanation and increasingly creative production can be generated at extraordinary scale. The marginal cost of many forms of cognitive output is approaching zero.

But abundance does not eliminate scarcity.

It **moves scarcity somewhere else**.

When answers become abundant, good questions become scarce.

When content becomes abundant, attention becomes scarce.

When intelligence becomes abundant, direction becomes scarce.

When synthetic output becomes abundant, verified contribution becomes scarce.

When autonomous agents become abundant, trustworthy identity, provenance and accountability become scarce.

When almost anything can be generated, knowing **what was actually done, by whom, for whom, and with what effect** becomes increasingly valuable.

This is the economic environment in which **Octomics** becomes important.

Octomics is the contribution layer of the Octonomous stack. It turns activity, learning and growth into visible, verifiable contribution and connects that contribution to useful resources. Activity is verified by trusted participants, anchored through KERI-based mechanisms, represented through Octos, and potentially exchanged through community exchanges.

Octomics therefore offers a different way of thinking about economics in the intelligence age.

Rather than asking:

> How do we manufacture scarcity so things retain economic value?

Octomics asks:

> What remains genuinely scarce, and how can verified contribution provide a fair path to it?

---

# 1. Economics Has Always Been About Scarcity

Traditional economics begins with a simple constraint.

Human wants are effectively unlimited while resources are limited.

Land is scarce.

Food can be scarce.

Labour is scarce.

Capital is scarce.

Machines are scarce.

Expertise is scarce.

Information has historically also been scarce.

Much of the industrial economy therefore developed systems for allocating these scarce resources.

Money became a general mechanism for representing claims upon them.

Markets became mechanisms for discovering relative scarcity.

Companies became mechanisms for coordinating scarce labour, capital and knowledge.

Educational credentials became mechanisms for signalling scarce expertise.

Intellectual property created artificial scarcity around reproducible information.

For centuries, scarcity existed throughout almost every layer of economic production.

The intelligence age changes this structure.

---

# 2. Intelligence Is Becoming Abundant

Generative AI begins removing scarcity from one of the most important inputs into economic activity:

**cognitive capability.**

A person can increasingly summon software development, research assistance, translation, analysis, tutoring, writing, visualisation and planning almost instantly.

An autonomous intelligent being may eventually be capable of performing thousands of cognitive tasks continuously.

This creates something historically unusual.

We may have extraordinarily abundant:

- intelligence
- information
- explanations
- simulations
- software
- designs
- media
- analysis
- optimisation
- recommendations
- digital labour

The selfdriven.you framework describes this shift directly: AI can increasingly provide answers, memory and execution, while human value moves toward determining what matters, creating meaning and building trust.

The important economic consequence is not simply that things become cheaper.

It is that **the location of scarcity changes**.

---

# 3. Abundance Moves the Bottleneck

Imagine a world in which a software application previously required ten engineers and six months to create.

If an intelligent system can produce the same software in minutes, software generation is no longer the primary constraint.

Something else becomes the bottleneck.

Perhaps:

- choosing the right problem;
- understanding the affected community;
- determining whether the software should exist;
- verifying the requirements;
- establishing trust;
- gaining permission;
- operating physical infrastructure;
- financing energy;
- obtaining regulatory approval;
- engaging people;
- maintaining relationships.

The constraint moves.

This produces a fundamental principle:

> **Every increase in abundance exposes the next layer of scarcity.**

AI does not create a post-scarcity world.

It creates a world in which scarcity becomes increasingly concentrated around things that cannot simply be generated.

---

# 4. What Becomes Scarce?

Several forms of scarcity become particularly important.

## Attention

When a billion pieces of content can be generated every minute, generating another piece of content has little intrinsic value.

Human attention remains finite.

Attention therefore becomes more valuable as information becomes abundant.

---

## Trust

AI can generate plausible claims almost without limit.

It cannot make every claim trustworthy.

The more abundant generated information becomes, the more valuable provenance becomes.

Who said this?

Who verified it?

What actually happened?

Can the claim be independently checked?

Trust becomes infrastructure.

---

## Direction

Abundant intelligence can answer enormous numbers of questions.

But intelligence does not automatically determine which questions deserve asking.

selfdriven.you describes humans increasingly as **direction setters, meaning makers and trust anchors**.

Direction becomes scarce precisely because execution becomes cheap.

---

## Physical Resources

Atoms remain different from bits.

Housing.

Land.

Food.

Water.

Energy.

Transport.

Computing hardware.

Medical equipment.

Rare materials.

Physical infrastructure.

These remain rivalrous resources.

AI may help us use them dramatically more efficiently, but they do not become infinitely reproducible merely because intelligence becomes abundant.

---

## Energy

Intelligence itself requires physical resources.

Computation requires electricity, hardware, cooling and infrastructure.

As intelligence becomes abundant, access to energy may become one of its primary physical constraints.

---

## Human Presence

A machine may generate a message.

It cannot manufacture another hour of someone's life.

Time spent:

- listening,
- caring,
- mentoring,
- participating,
- helping,
- playing,
- teaching,
- building community

remains intrinsically scarce.

This makes genuine human engagement increasingly valuable.

---

## Responsibility

Millions of agents may be capable of proposing an action.

Far fewer entities may be willing and authorised to take responsibility for the consequences.

Responsibility becomes scarce.

---

## Verified Contribution

AI makes generating the appearance of work extraordinarily cheap.

Documents can be generated.

Reports can be generated.

Code can be generated.

Images can be generated.

Evidence of **actual contribution**, however, remains fundamentally different.

Did someone learn?

Did something improve?

Was someone helped?

Was a problem solved?

Did the community value the result?

Was the work verified?

This is where Octomics enters.

---

# 5. Octomics: Economics After Output Scarcity

Octomics describes itself as the **contribution layer** within the Octonomous stack.

Its basic loop is:

**Activity → Verification → Octos → Useful Resources**

Real activity and growth are captured through systems such as selfdrivenOS and Level Up. Trusted learning partners verify that activity. KERI provides a tamper-evident identity and provenance foundation. Verified contribution can then be represented through Octos and connected through community exchanges to useful resources.

This is significant because the system does not primarily reward the production of information.

It seeks to recognise **contribution**.

That distinction becomes increasingly important in an abundant intelligence economy.

An AI could produce 10,000 pages.

That does not mean 10,000 pages of value were created.

A person might instead:

- help a neighbour repair something;
- mentor a teenager;
- restore a community garden;
- learn a difficult new skill;
- resolve a dispute;
- improve an open-source project;
- care for another person;
- remove friction from a community process.

These activities may generate relatively little digital output.

Yet they may create enormous real value.

Octomics allows economics to move its unit of attention from:

**output**

toward:

**verified contribution**.

# 6. Octos Are Not About Artificial Scarcity

One temptation in digital economics has been to recreate old scarcity inside digital environments.

A digitally reproducible object can be made artificially scarce.

Only 10,000 exist.

Only one person owns this token.

Only a limited number will ever be minted.

Artificial scarcity can sometimes be useful.

But it is not the deepest opportunity presented by the intelligence age.

Octomics points toward something different.

The scarce object does not need to be the token.

The scarce thing is the **underlying contribution**.

Someone performed an activity.

Someone learned.

Someone helped.

Someone created something useful.

Someone verified that activity.

That event occurred in reality.

Octomics describes Octos as a portable, KERI-based representation of contribution, issued following verification rather than as a purely speculative asset.

The Octo is therefore better understood as a representation of an event in the contribution graph.

It says, effectively:

**something happened here.**

That is very different from:

**we have created a scarce digital object and hope someone values it.**

# 8. Scarcity Becomes Contextual

Traditional money is deliberately context-poor.

A dollar generally does not know:

- why it was earned;
- how it was earned;
- what activity produced it;
- what capability was developed;
- who verified the activity;
- which community it came from.

This abstraction is enormously useful.

But abstraction also removes context.

Octomics can operate differently.

Contribution can exist within a graph.

A contribution might be connected to:

**Person  
→ Activity  
→ Skill  
→ Community  
→ Verification  
→ Outcome  
→ Octo  
→ Resource**

Economic value becomes contextual.

This allows communities to reason about scarcity locally.

A rural community may need nurses.

Another may need housing.

Another may need mentors.

Another may need ecological restoration.

Another may need software engineers.

There is no universal scarcity function.

Scarcity is contextual.

Octomics therefore does not need to answer:

> What is valuable?

for the entire world.

Communities can answer:

> What is useful here, now?

# 10. The 4Cs Become Economic Primitives

Octomics is built upon the selfdriven 4Cs:

**Curious  
Caring  
Constructive  
Chill.**

These might initially appear cultural rather than economic.

In an abundance economy they become surprisingly important economic primitives.

## Curious

When answers are abundant, **questions become scarce**.

Curiosity determines where intelligence is directed.

## Caring

When interactions can be generated artificially at enormous scale, **trusted relationships remain scarce**.

Caring creates social trust.

## Constructive

When ideas and commentary are abundant, **actual improvements remain scarce**.

Constructiveness converts intelligence into reality.

When systems can operate continuously at machine speed, **sustainable human attention becomes scarce**.

Chill protects the capacity to participate over time.

selfdriven.you describes this precisely as a rhythm for remaining coherent as intelligence accelerates.

The 4Cs therefore act as a behavioural architecture around the scarce human qualities that abundant machine intelligence does not automatically provide.

# 12. The Scarcity Gradient

We can think of economic transformation as a **scarcity gradient**.

As each layer becomes abundant, economic importance moves toward the next constraint.

For example:

**Information scarcity  
↓  
Intelligence scarcity  
↓  
Execution scarcity  
↓  
Direction scarcity  
↓  
Trust scarcity  
↓  
Contribution scarcity  
↓  
Physical-resource scarcity**

AI is pushing us rapidly downward through this gradient.

Most organisations are still operating near the top.

They continue measuring:

- documents produced;
- hours worked;
- emails sent;
- lines of code;
- meetings attended;
- content generated.

Yet these outputs are rapidly becoming abundant.

The economically meaningful question moves further down the stack:

> **What changed?**

That is the Octomics question.

---

# 13. From Productivity to Contribution

Industrial economics became obsessed with productivity:

**output / input**

Produce more units with fewer resources.

AI may eventually make enormous parts of digital productivity almost meaningless as a measurement.

If one autonomous being can generate ten thousand software components overnight, counting components tells us very little.

A more useful measure becomes:

**contribution / resources consumed**

or perhaps:

**positive change / resources consumed**

This reframes economic efficiency.

The goal is no longer simply to produce more.

The goal becomes:

> Create the greatest useful change from the resources available.

Octomics provides a possible accounting layer for that transition.

---

# 14. Abundance Makes Verification More Important, Not Less

There is a paradox at the heart of abundance.

The easier something becomes to produce, the harder it becomes to infer meaning from its existence.

A handwritten twenty-page report once implied substantial effort.

A twenty-page report generated in seconds implies almost nothing about effort, knowledge or understanding.

The artefact loses signalling power.

Verification must therefore move closer to the underlying activity.

Instead of asking:

> Did you produce the document?

we ask:

> What did you understand?

> What did you change?

> What did you contribute?

> Who can verify it?

> What outcome followed?

Octomics makes this transition explicit through trusted verification and KERI-anchored representations of activity and growth.

In the age of synthetic abundance:

**provenance becomes more valuable than production.**

---

# 15. The Paradox of the Abundance Economy

The intelligence age may create the greatest abundance humanity has experienced.

Yet this abundance will make genuine scarcity more visible than ever.

Infinite content highlights finite attention.

Infinite answers highlight finite curiosity.

Infinite agents highlight finite responsibility.

Infinite synthetic interaction highlights finite human presence.

Infinite possible actions highlight finite direction.

Infinite digital production highlights finite physical resources.

And infinite claims highlight finite trust.

Octomics matters because it sits directly on this boundary.

It provides a mechanism for communities to recognise activity, verify growth, represent contribution and connect that contribution to resources that remain genuinely useful.

It does not attempt to preserve the old economy by manufacturing scarcity around abundant things.

It asks something more interesting:

> **What remains scarce when intelligence becomes abundant?**

And then:

> **How do we help people and communities gain access to those scarce resources through meaningful contribution?**

---

# Conclusion

The age of abundance is not the end of economics.

It may be the beginning of a more precise economics.

The industrial economy allocated scarce physical production.

The information economy allocated scarce information and attention.

The intelligence economy will increasingly need to allocate around scarcer and more fundamental things:

**trust, direction, attention, energy, physical resources, responsibility, human connection and verified contribution.**

Octomics provides a framework for this transition.

It begins with real activity.

It makes growth visible.

It makes contribution verifiable.

It allows that contribution to become portable.

And it connects contribution back to useful resources.

The economic loop therefore becomes:

**Abundant Intelligence  
→ Intent  
→ Activity  
→ Verified Contribution  
→ Access to Scarce Resources  
→ Further Growth**

The defining economic question of the intelligence age may therefore not be:

> **What can we produce?**

Increasingly, the answer will be:

**almost anything.**

The important questions become:

> **What matters?**

> **What actually happened?**

> **Who contributed?**

> **What remains scarce?**

> **And how should those scarce resources be directed toward further growth?**

That is the space Octomics is designed to occupy.

**Abundance changes what is cheap.  
Scarcity reveals what matters.  
Contribution connects the two.**
