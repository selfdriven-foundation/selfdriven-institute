# Beyond Left and Right  
## How selfdriven Frameworks Can Help Reduce Political Polarisation

### Abstract

Political polarisation is often treated as a problem of disagreement.

Perhaps it is more fundamentally a problem of **how people relate to themselves, to one another, and to their communities**.

Healthy societies have always contained disagreement. Different interests, philosophies, values and visions of the future are necessary features of a functioning democracy. The problem begins when disagreement becomes identity: when a political position stops being something a person holds and becomes something a person **is**.

At that point, changing one's mind can feel like losing oneself. Listening can feel like surrender. Compromise can feel like betrayal. The person on the other side is no longer someone with a different model of the world; they become a threat.

Research increasingly describes this phenomenon as *affective polarisation*: hostility towards people associated with another political group rather than merely disagreement with their policies. Partisan identity can become a powerful social identity extending far beyond politics.

The selfdriven frameworks offer a different point of intervention.

Rather than attempting to tell people **what to think politically**, selfdriven focuses on strengthening the human capacities that allow people to live alongside difference:

**Curiosity.  
Caring.  
Constructiveness.  
Chill.**

selfdriven.you describes these four modes as a sustainable human operating rhythm: curiosity opens possibilities, caring builds trust, constructiveness turns potential into contribution, and chill restores the capacity to participate coherently.

Combined with selfdriven's broader focus on self-actuation, community projects, reflection, relationships, contribution, identity and distributed governance, these principles suggest an alternative approach to political polarisation.

The goal is not to make everyone agree.

The goal is to make disagreement **safe again**.

---

# 1. Polarisation Is Not the Same as Disagreement

Two people can strongly disagree and still:

- respect one another;
- work together;
- share resources;
- solve practical problems;
- recognise each other's humanity;
- change their minds;
- live peacefully within the same community.

That is pluralism.

Polarisation becomes dangerous when disagreement moves from:

**“I think you are wrong.”**

to:

**“People like you are the problem.”**

The political issue has moved from the **idea layer** into the **identity layer**.

This distinction matters enormously.

Ideas can be examined.

Identities are defended.

Once political affiliation becomes a major component of personal identity, ordinary cognitive processes begin protecting the group. Information from one's own group is interpreted generously. Information from another group is treated suspiciously. Motives are attributed differently. Ambiguity is resolved against the opponent.

Politics gradually becomes:

> **Us versus them.**

The problem is therefore not simply misinformation.

It is **identity compression**.

A rich human being — parent, neighbour, gardener, engineer, musician, volunteer, learner, football supporter, maker, friend — becomes compressed into:

**Left.**

or:

**Right.**

Progressive.

Conservative.

Globalist.

Nationalist.

Urban.

Regional.

Elite.

Working class.

Once this compression occurs, an enormous amount of information about the actual human disappears.

---

# 2. The Political Layer Has Swallowed the Human Layer

Modern communication systems intensify this compression.

Digital platforms need to decide what deserves attention.

Engagement provides an easy signal.

Unfortunately, anger, threat, outrage and conflict are powerful generators of engagement.

Research has found that observers systematically overestimate the amount of moral outrage expressed by others online, causing people to perceive groups as more hostile and extreme than they actually are.

A large 2026 experiment examining social-media feed design found that engagement-based ranking amplified intergroup, moralised, emotional and toxic content and increased perceptions of partisan animosity. Reducing the influence of extreme users improved people's perception of social norms without substantially reducing enjoyment of the platform.

This creates a profound distortion.

The citizen increasingly encounters the **most politically activated representation of another group**, rather than ordinary members of that group.

We stop encountering:

> Bob, who disagrees with me about immigration but coaches junior football every Saturday.

Instead we encounter:

> ANOTHER EXAMPLE OF WHAT THESE PEOPLE BELIEVE.

Humans become categories.

Categories become caricatures.

Caricatures become enemies.

---

# 3. The Missing Layer Is Human Agency

There is another factor underneath polarisation: **loss of agency**.

A person who feels capable of changing their immediate world has somewhere productive to put their energy.

They can:

- start something;
- build something;
- help someone;
- teach;
- repair;
- organise;
- learn;
- create;
- participate.

A person who feels powerless has fewer pathways.

Politics can then become one of the only available mechanisms through which frustration, identity, purpose and belonging can be expressed.

The individual becomes a spectator to enormous systems.

Housing.

Climate.

Migration.

War.

Automation.

Healthcare.

Education.

Artificial intelligence.

Economic inequality.

The problems are enormous; the individual feels tiny.

One response is helplessness.

Another is outrage.

Another is finding a group that explains who is responsible.

This makes **self-actuation politically important without being political**.

The selfdriven Foundation describes its purpose as helping communities self-actuate: giving communities agency to organise, grow and pursue shared goals rather than relying entirely upon top-down systems.

That principle may have an important civic consequence:

> **People who can act together have less need to fight symbolically against one another.**

# 4. selfdriven.you: A Human Operating Model for Pluralism

selfdriven.you proposes four mutually supporting modes:

## Curious  
## Caring  
## Constructive  
## Chill

They were not designed as a political ideology.

That may be exactly why they are useful.

They operate **one level below ideology**.

They describe how people engage with reality rather than prescribing the political conclusions they should reach.

---

# 5. Curious: From Certainty to Inquiry

Polarisation thrives on certainty.

> They are stupid.

> They are evil.

> They hate people like us.

> Everyone on that side believes this.

Curiosity inserts one of the most powerful questions available to a human being:

> **What if my model is incomplete?**

selfdriven.you encourages practices such as noticing assumptions, exploring uncertainty and genuinely attempting to understand why an intelligent person might hold an unfamiliar view.

This does not require abandoning one's beliefs.

Quite the opposite.

A belief that cannot survive curiosity is fragile.

Curiosity allows someone to say:

> I disagree with you.  
> But I want to understand how you got there.

That seemingly small shift transforms the relationship.

The other person stops being an object to defeat and becomes a source of information about reality.

### Curiosity changes:

**“How can anyone believe that?”**

into:

**“What experiences could cause someone to believe that?”**

That question is one of the foundations of pluralistic society.

---

# 6. Caring: From Political Object to Human Being

Curiosity makes understanding possible.

Caring makes relationship possible.

selfdriven places relationships at the centre of community life and describes strong communities as environments where people feel seen, heard and valued.

Caring does not mean agreement.

You can care deeply about someone while thinking they are profoundly mistaken.

This distinction has become increasingly important.

Political discourse frequently treats empathy as endorsement:

> If you understand them, you must support them.

That is false.

Understanding is simply higher-resolution information.

Caring allows two statements to coexist:

> **I think your position is wrong.**

and:

> **I care what happens to you.**

A healthy democracy requires both.

Research into cross-partisan contact supports the importance of this relational layer. Experiments have found reductions in polarisation after civil discussions between political opponents, including surprisingly brief conversations.

The objective therefore need not be:

> Make everyone think alike.

A much more achievable objective is:

> **Increase the number of people with whom disagreement remains compatible with care.**

---

# 7. Constructive: From Fighting About Society to Building Society

Perhaps the most distinctive selfdriven contribution is **Constructive**.

Political systems train citizens to express preferences.

Community systems can train citizens to create outcomes.

There is an enormous difference.

One asks:

> What do you think should happen?

The other asks:

> **What can we make happen?**

selfdriven defines constructiveness through contribution: building useful things, creating clarity, sharing knowledge and reducing friction.

Imagine two neighbours with opposing political views.

Ask them to debate national energy policy and political identities may immediately activate.

Ask them instead to help reduce the electricity cost of their local sporting club and something different happens.

They examine roofs.

They calculate consumption.

They talk to suppliers.

They investigate grants.

They compare technologies.

They build a spreadsheet.

They make a decision.

They achieve something.

Political identity has not disappeared.

It has simply ceased to be the highest-resolution description of the relationship.

They are now:

> **two people solving a problem together.**

Research provides encouraging evidence for precisely this mechanism. Experiments involving cooperative interaction between opposing partisans have found reductions in negative partisanship, with one cooperative game intervention producing effects lasting for months.

The antidote to an enemy is not necessarily an argument.

Sometimes it is **a shared project**.

---

# 8. Chill: Lowering the Temperature of Democracy

This fourth mode may appear the least political.

It may be one of the most important.

Polarised systems operate at high emotional velocity.

Notifications.

Breaking news.

Reaction.

Counter-reaction.

Outrage.

Response.

Quote-post.

Attack.

Defend.

Repeat.

The nervous system rarely receives enough time to return to equilibrium.

selfdriven.you describes Chill not as disengagement or laziness but as **nervous-system sustainability**: rest, low-stimulation spaces, unstructured time and slower conversation.

The significance for political discourse is substantial.

A person does not have the same conversation at:

**high arousal**

as they do at:

**low arousal**.

Chill introduces space between stimulus and response.

That creates room for:

- interpretation;
- context;
- curiosity;
- empathy;
- reflection;
- uncertainty.

A healthy civic system therefore needs more than freedom of speech.

It needs **space between speeches**.

---

The four modes form a useful civic loop:

### Curious

**What am I missing?**

↓

### Caring

**Who is affected?**

↓

### Constructive

**What can we do together?**

↓

### Chill

**Can we slow down, reflect and remain coherent?**

↓

### Curious again

**What have we learned?**

This differs radically from the engagement loop dominating many digital environments:

### Trigger

↓

### React

↓

### Signal allegiance

↓

### Receive reinforcement

↓

### Find another trigger

One generates learning.

The other generates polarisation.

# 10. Move Identity From Politics Toward Contribution

A powerful selfdriven principle is recognition of growth and contribution.

This suggests another mechanism for reducing polarisation.

People naturally seek status and recognition.

The question is:

> **What does the system reward?**

A political social network may reward:

- certainty;
- allegiance;
- outrage;
- virality;
- humiliation of opponents.

A self-actuating community can instead recognise:

- contribution;
- mentoring;
- collaboration;
- problem solving;
- learning;
- reflection;
- reliability;
- caring;
- creating useful things.

This changes the social status game.

Imagine if one of the strongest identity signals in a community was not:

> **What political side are you on?**

but:

> **What have you helped with?**

The result is not an apolitical society.

It is a society where politics becomes **one dimension of identity rather than the container for identity**.

---

# 11. Build Cross-Cutting Identities

One reason political identity becomes dangerous is that multiple identities can align into a single axis.

Political affiliation starts predicting:

- where someone lives;
- what media they consume;
- what they believe;
- who their friends are;
- what organisations they join;
- sometimes even what products they purchase.

Society becomes easier to divide into two enormous clusters.

Community activity can create **cross-cutting identities**.

Someone might simultaneously be:

- economically conservative;
- environmentally active;
- a football coach;
- a volunteer firefighter;
- an open-source contributor;
- a parent;
- a local-history enthusiast;
- a community-garden member.

Now the graph becomes much harder to split neatly.

selfdriven's emphasis on projects, learning communities and relationships naturally encourages these overlapping connections.

Every shared project adds another edge between people.

Those edges matter.

A society with millions of weak cross-group edges is structurally different from a society consisting of two internally dense but mutually disconnected clusters.

Political resilience may therefore be partly understood as a **graph problem**.

The objective is not to remove communities.

It is to increase the number of meaningful edges **between** them.

---

# 12. From Debate Communities to Action Communities

Traditional civic engagement often begins with conversation.

selfdriven suggests beginning with **activity**.

Instead of creating a forum called:

> Political Dialogue Between Left and Right

create a project called:

> Make Our Local Park Better.

Bring together whoever cares.

Let political diversity occur naturally inside a shared purpose.

The participants may eventually discuss:

- public spending;
- accessibility;
- environmental priorities;
- policing;
- youth services;
- planning;
- governance.

But now these discussions take place between people who already possess a shared identity:

> **We are working on this together.**

This changes the starting condition of disagreement.

Contact research has long suggested that cooperation, common goals and roughly equal status can reduce intergroup hostility, and contemporary political-polarisation experiments continue to find promising effects from such interactions.

selfdriven therefore offers a potential shift from:

**politics → community**

towards:

**community → governance**.

That direction matters.

# 13. selfdriven Communities as Civic Infrastructure

selfdriven's broader framework adds several structural layers beyond the individual's 4Cs.

Its governance approach emphasises supporting community initiatives, recognising contribution, growing capacity, strengthening local identity and making decisions together.

These can form the basis of a civic operating model:

### 1. Identity

People arrive as whole humans rather than political categories.

### 2. Purpose

Communities identify what matters locally.

### 3. Projects

Members act on those priorities together.

### 4. Contribution

Useful activity becomes visible and recognised.

### 5. Reflection

Communities examine what happened and what was learned.

### 6. Governance

Decisions emerge around actual shared activity.

### 7. Growth

People increase their capability to participate.

### 8. Connection

New relationships make the community graph denser and more resilient.

Political discussion becomes embedded within lived cooperation rather than occurring primarily as abstract ideological combat.

# 14. AI Could Either Intensify or Reduce Polarisation

Artificial intelligence introduces another critical dimension.

AI could make political persuasion radically cheaper.

Millions of personalised messages can be generated, tested and adapted.

Synthetic people can participate in discussions.

Emotional vulnerabilities can potentially be identified.

Narratives can be generated at industrial scale.

Without appropriate human governance, AI could therefore create an extraordinary amplification mechanism for political polarisation.

But the opposite is also possible.

selfdriven AI describes a **Human Conductor** model in which humans set intent and retain accountability while AI assists within defined boundaries.

Applied to civic interaction, AI should not be designed to tell people which political beliefs to adopt.

Instead, it could act as **cognitive assistance for pluralism**.

For example:

> **What assumptions are being made by each side?**

> **Which claims appear factual and which are value judgements?**

> **Where do the participants actually agree?**

> **Can you restate this person's argument in a form they would recognise?**

> **What evidence would change either position?**

> **What local action is compatible with both sets of values?**

> **What are the uncertainties?**

> **Can the temperature of this conversation be lowered without suppressing disagreement?**

AI then becomes a tool for **epistemological friction** rather than frictionless persuasion.

Its job is not:

> Win.

Its job becomes:

> **Help the humans see more of the space.**

# 16. From Affective Polarisation to Constructive Pluralism

The objective should not necessarily be **depolarisation** if that means removing meaningful differences.

Some issues really are important.

Some positions really are incompatible.

Some disagreements cannot be solved by finding a comfortable middle.

Democracy needs passionate disagreement.

A better goal is:

# Constructive Pluralism

A constructively pluralistic society can contain strong disagreement while maintaining:

- human dignity;
- curiosity;
- trust;
- peaceful cooperation;
- institutional legitimacy;
- local agency;
- willingness to learn;
- capacity to change;
- common spaces.

Political difference remains.

Political dehumanisation does not.

---

# 17. What selfdriven Does Not Attempt to Do

selfdriven should not become another political ideology.

Doing so would destroy much of its value.

It does not need to define the correct position on:

- taxation;
- immigration;
- climate policy;
- defence;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- regulation;
- welfare.

Instead it can protect something more fundamental:

> **the conditions under which humans can work through those questions together.**

Nor should the evidence be overstated.

Research shows that interventions reducing interpersonal partisan hostility do not necessarily reduce support for anti-democratic behaviour. Improving relationships is important but is not a substitute for functioning institutions, democratic norms, constitutional protections or political accountability.

selfdriven should therefore be understood as **civic substrate**, not a complete theory of democracy.

It strengthens the human and community layer upon which democratic institutions depend.

# 18. The Deeper Shift: From Opinion to Agency

The modern citizen is constantly asked:

> What do you think?

> Which side are you on?

> Who do you support?

> Who do you oppose?

selfdriven asks a different question:

> **What are you curious about?**

> **Who do you care about?**

> **What can you construct?**

> **How do you remain coherent enough to continue?**

These questions produce a very different society.

They move the individual from:

**consumer → participant**

**spectator → actor**

**opponent → collaborator**

**identity → multidimensional human**

**outrage → agency**

**position → contribution**

---

# 19. Politics Becomes Smaller Because Humanity Becomes Larger

Perhaps the ultimate objective is not to remove politics from society.

It is to stop politics occupying so much of the available human identity space.

A person should be able to say:

> I disagree with you about this.

without meaning:

> I reject you.

A community should be able to contain competing models of the future without fragmenting into mutually hostile tribes.

The selfdriven frameworks provide a possible way of rebuilding that capacity.

Not through a new ideology.

Not through censorship.

Not through forced consensus.

Not by asking everyone to move towards an artificial political centre.

But through something more foundational:

**Curiosity that keeps our models open.**

**Caring that keeps other people human.**

**Constructiveness that gives us things worth doing together.**

**Chill that gives us enough cognitive and emotional space to continue.**

And beneath all four:

**agency.**

The ability of individuals and communities to act upon the world they inhabit.

---

# Conclusion

Political polarisation is often presented as a battle of ideas.

Increasingly, it is a battle of identities amplified by systems optimised for engagement.

Trying to win that battle with still more information, still more persuasion and still more political messaging may simply add energy to the system producing the problem.

selfdriven offers another path.

Start with the person.

Strengthen agency.

Restore curiosity.

Build relationships.

Create things together.

Recognise contribution.

Slow interaction down.

Create overlapping communities.

Let governance emerge from humans who already have experience working alongside one another.

The purpose is not consensus.

It is something both harder and more valuable:

> **the ability to remain connected while disagreeing.**

In an increasingly polarised society, that may become one of the most important capabilities communities can develop.

And in an age in which artificial intelligence makes information, argument and persuasion almost infinitely abundant, the scarce resource may no longer be intelligence.

It may be the human capacity to remain:

**Curious.  
Caring.  
Constructive.  
Chill.**

Together.

---

## Central Proposition

> **Political polarisation cannot be solved only at the political layer.**
>
> It must also be addressed at the human and community layers from which politics emerges.
>
> selfdriven helps people move from **identity through opposition** toward **identity through agency, relationship and contribution**.
>
> We do not need a society without disagreement.
>
> **We need a society capable of disagreement without disintegration.**

## References and Research Basis

The selfdriven.you Human OS and its Curious, Caring, Constructive and Chill modes provide the primary framework used in this paper.

The selfdriven Foundation's community model emphasises self-actuation, relationships, reflection, growth, contribution and shared governance.

Research on affective polarisation identifies partisan identity and intergroup animosity as important dimensions of modern political division.
